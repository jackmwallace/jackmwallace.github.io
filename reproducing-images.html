    5. Images in the book

    The process decided where a picture could go. A relief block is type-high and prints with the text; an intaglio plate or a lithographic stone needs a different press and usually a separate leaf. These differences leave evidence a bibliographer must record.

    In a bibliographical description

    • Woodcuts, wood engravings and line or halftone blocks printed with the text are part of the gatherings. Note them in the contents and, for woodcuts, identify the blocks (for early English books, Hodnett).
    • Plates printed separately, in intaglio or lithography, are not part of the collational formula. List them separately with their position, process and plate-mark size. Check whether each is a singleton tipped or guarded in, or conjugate with another plate.
    • Record the lettering below an intaglio plate exactly. "Pinx." or "delin." names the designer and "sculp." or "fecit" the engraver; lettering added or changed marks a new state of the plate.
    • Watch for offsets (ink transferred onto a facing page), tissue guards, plate numbers and directions to the binder, which show where plates were meant to go.
