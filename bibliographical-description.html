Ideal copy and this copy

An ideal copy description describes the edition (or a particular impression or issue) as the printer and publisher intended it to be issued, reconstructed from as many copies as you can examine. A copy that lacks a leaf does not change the formula; the lack is recorded as an imperfection of that copy. Bowers set out the concept, and Tanselle refined it as a historical reconstruction of the form or forms in which the book was published. A copy description records what is particular to one copy: its size after trimming, imperfections, binding, provenance and annotations. A full record gives the ideal copy first and then notes on each copy examined.

1Quasi-facsimile transcription of the title page

Edition
How to transcribe
  • Transcribe every word exactly as printed: spelling, punctuation and capitalisation, with nothing omitted or corrected.
  • Mark each line ending with a vertical stroke |.
  • Distinguish the founts: roman, italic, small capitals and black letter. Use the buttons below, or another convention stated in your introduction.
  • Describe anything that is not type in square brackets: [rule], [double rule], [ornament], [printer's device], [woodcut], with a reference where possible (for devices, McKerrow).
  • Keep i/j and u/v as printed. Keep or normalise long s (ſ) and ligatures, and say which you have done. Mark words printed in red.
  • Transcribe half-titles, divisional title pages and colophons the same way.

2Format and collational formula

Edition
How to write the formula
  • Give the format from the evidence of chain lines and watermarks, not from size: 2°, 4°, 8°, 12°. Note quiring or half-sheet imposition, as in 4° (in 8s).
  • Write the register of gatherings in Bowers's notation: the 23-letter alphabet (no J, U or W), a superior figure for the leaves in each gathering, ranges such as B–M8, 2A for doubled letters, and a superior figure before the letter for a repeated series (2A).
  • Use π for unsigned preliminaries outside the series and χ for unsigned gatherings elsewhere. Print an inferred signature in italic, or in square brackets.
  • Show alterations after the gathering: (±A3) for a cancel, (−N4) for a leaf removed, (A2+χ1) for a leaf inserted.
  • Describe the ideal copy. Separately printed plates are not part of the formula; list them under Plates.

Type superscripts with a caret: A^4 B–M^8 N^4 becomes A4 B–M8 N4. For a fuller tool, use the Collational Formula Workbench.

3Signing statement, pagination and contents

Edition
How to record signing, pagination and contents
  • Signing statement: $ and the number of leaves normally signed in each gathering, with exceptions in parentheses: $4 signed (−A1, N3–4). Record missigning in words: K3 signed K2.
  • Give the total of leaves, then the pagination as printed. Unnumbered pages go in square brackets, either as a count, [8], or as inferred numbers, [i–viii]. Keep roman numerals as printed, and use ff. for a book numbered by leaves.
  • Record errors after the pagination: (p. 89 misnumbered 98).
  • Contents: list the parts of the book leaf by leaf, by signature, with recto and verso: A1r title; A1v blank; A2r–A4v The Preface. Note blanks, which belong to the ideal copy even when a copy lacks them.

4Inserted plates

Edition
How to describe plates
  • Plates are leaves printed separately, usually from engraved or etched copper, and inserted. They are not counted in the formula.
  • For each plate give its subject, where it is placed (frontispiece, facing p. 64, after K4), the process, the size of the plate-mark (height × width in mm), and any signatures of artist or engraver as printed (pinx., delin., sculp.).
  • Note folding plates, plates printed on both sides, and plates that copies commonly lack.

5Measurements and format

Edition and copy
What to measure
  • Leaf size belongs to the copy, since every binder trimmed differently. Give height × width in millimetres and say whether the edges are cut, trimmed or uncut.
  • Type measurements belong to the edition. Give the depth of 20 lines of solid text, from the foot of one line to the foot of the line twenty lines below, and the size of the type page on a named page.
  • Name the typeface and type style (roman, italic, black letter) of the text, and the number of lines in a full page.

6Paper

Edition and copy
How to describe the paper
  • Say whether the paper is laid or wove. Wove paper appears in England from the 1750s.
  • Chain-line direction confirms the format: normally vertical in folio and octavo, horizontal in quarto and ordinary duodecimo. Measure the distance between chain lines in millimetres.
  • Describe the watermark and any countermark, where they fall in the gathering, and compare them with repertories such as Briquet, Heawood or Gravell.
  • Record each paper stock separately with the gatherings it is used for. A change of paper can show interrupted printing, a cancel, or sheets reissued from another impression.
  • The leaf size gives only a minimum for the size of the sheet, because the leaves have been trimmed.

7Provenance

This copy
What to record
  • Record all evidence of ownership and use: inscriptions, bookplates, stamps, shelfmarks, prices and sale codes, annotations, and the binding itself.
  • Transcribe inscriptions exactly, say where they are (title, upper margin; front pastedown), and date them by hand if they are undated.
  • Identify owners where you can and give your evidence. Note evidence that has been removed: erasures, a bookplate taken out, a torn-away corner.
  • List provenance in chronological order, earliest first.

8Ornaments, initials and devices

Edition
How to describe ornaments
  • Record headpieces, tailpieces, factotums, decorated initials, printers' devices and type ornaments, with their location, size (height × width in mm) and a short description.
  • Identify them from standard references (McKerrow for devices) and from other books by the same printer. Woodcut blocks crack and wear, so the state of a block can help to date a printing and to identify an unnamed printer.

9Running titles, catchwords and press figures

Edition
How to record running titles
  • Transcribe the running titles (headlines) on versos and rectos, noting where they change.
  • Variant settings and damaged types in the running titles can show how many skeleton formes were used and the order in which sheets went through the press. This is the basis of Bowers's skeleton-forme analysis.
  • Note irregular catchwords, and, for eighteenth-century English books, the press figures and where they appear.

10Binding

This copy
How to describe a binding
  • Hand-press bindings belong to the copy. Publishers' edition bindings, such as cloth from the later 1820s on, belong to the edition; tick the box below if that applies.
  • Work from the inside out or the outside in, consistently: covering material and colour; boards; decoration of the covers (fillets, rolls, panels, tools, in blind or gilt); spine (bands, compartments, labels, lettering); edges; endleaves; sewing and structure where visible; fastenings; condition and repairs.
  • Say whether the binding is contemporary, and why. Record any binder's ticket or stamp. The Bookbinding Structures Workbench gives the terms.

11Notes on the edition

Edition
What belongs here
  • The printing and publication history: printer and publisher (from the imprint, colophon, registers such as the Stationers' Register, newspaper advertisements and term catalogues), date and price.
  • The edition's relation to others: reprints, page-for-page resettings, shared standing type.
  • Impressions, issues and states, with the evidence for each: cancels, variant title pages, press corrections. Keep Bowers's and Tanselle's distinctions between edition, impression, issue and state.

12References, copies and other notes

Edition and copy
References and copies
  • Cite the standard catalogues: the ESTC citation number (a letter prefix and number), and STC (to 1640) or Wing (1641–1700) numbers where they apply; specialist bibliographies; and library catalogue records.
  • An ideal-copy description lists the copies examined, with their locations and shelfmarks.
  • For this copy, record where it is, its shelfmark, and its imperfections: leaves lacking, damaged or supplied from another copy.
