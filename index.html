Collational Formula Workbench

A guide to Fredson Bowers's collational formula. Choose a format, gather gatherings from a signature series, mark any cancels, insertions or signing irregularities, then bind the book. The workbench writes the formula, the signing statement and the leaf count.

Reading the formula

2° 4° 8° 12°
Format: folio, quarto, octavo, duodecimo. Format describes how the sheet was folded, not the size of the book. Quiring or half-sheet imposition that changes the normal gathering is noted in parentheses, as in 4° (in 8s) or 8° (in 4s).
A–Z4
Every gathering from A to Z has the number of leaves shown by the superior figure. The run uses the 23-letter alphabet, so A–Z4 is 23 gatherings and 92 leaves. If J, U or W were used, the formula says so.
2A, 3A
A doubled or tripled signature (Aa or AA, Aaa or AAA) in a second or third run through the alphabet. A–2C8 runs straight through Z into 2A, 2B and 2C.
2A
A superior figure in front marks a repeated series: the printer started the alphabet again with the same single letters, as often happens in a second part or an appendix.
a4 *4 ¶4
Lower-case letters and printers' symbols, often used for preliminaries printed last. A doubled symbol (** or ¶¶) is written 2* or 2¶.
A4, [A]4
A gathering with no printed signature whose letter can be inferred from its place in the series. Bowers prints the inferred signature in italic. Many catalogues, which cannot print italic, put it in square brackets instead.
π χ
Unsigned gatherings that cannot be fitted into the series. π marks preliminaries before the main sequence. χ marks unsigned gatherings elsewhere, such as inserted plates or text. A second one becomes 2π or 2χ.
D4(±D2)
A cancel: the original leaf (the cancellandum) was cut away and a replacement (the cancellans) was pasted onto its stub.
K4(−K4)
A leaf removed without replacement, often a blank. The superior figure still counts the leaves the gathering was made with.
F4(F2+χ1)
A single leaf inserted, here after F2.
$3 signed (−A1)
The statement of signing. $ means each gathering, and the figure is how many leaves in each normally carry a signature. Exceptions follow in parentheses: − for leaves within the $ figure that are unsigned, + for extra leaves that are signed. Missigned leaves can be added in words, such as "K3 signed K2".
Based on Fredson Bowers, Principles of Bibliographical Description (1949), and Philip Gaskell, A New Introduction to Bibliography (1972). The diagrams draw each gathering's conjugate leaves as nested pairs, shaded by the sheet they came from.
